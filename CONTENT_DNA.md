# Pollen Data Content DNA

Version: 1.0  
Date: 2026-04-16  
Status: Approved baseline for website content direction

## 1) Business Goal

Pollen Data exists to help organizations convert fragmented, low-trust data into business capabilities that improve decisions, operations, and revenue.

Primary business goal for the website:
- Generate qualified assessment-driven pipeline that converts into long-term platform and data product engagements.

Commercial intent:
- Sell business outcomes and capabilities, not generic consulting hours.

## 2) Core Positioning

Pollen Data is a data partner that moves companies through a clear journey:
- Data chaos
- Data foundation
- Data products with measurable business value

Positioning line:
- Turn your data into a business asset, not a cost center.

Category framing:
- Outcome-led data transformation partner with product mindset.

## 3) Ideal Customer Profiles (ICP)

Primary ICP (EN):
- Mid-market and enterprise organizations with multiple core systems (ERP, CRM, operations, finance, support).
- Decision makers: CIO, CDO, COO, VP Data, Head of BI, transformation leaders.

Secondary ICP (HE/Israel):
- Local businesses with legacy reporting/processes and limited internal data engineering capacity.
- Decision makers: Owners, GMs, operations managers, finance leaders.

Buying trigger signals:
- Conflicting reports for the same KPI.
- Slow analytics delivery.
- Heavy spreadsheet dependency.
- Integration breaks during system changes.
- No clear ownership of business definitions.

## 4) Problem Narrative

Core problem:
- Data is trapped inside systems instead of managed as an independent business layer.

Business consequences:
- Slow and costly decisions.
- Low trust in reporting.
- Rework across teams.
- Inability to scale analytics and automation.
- Missed monetization opportunities.

Tone rule:
- Speak in business impact first, architecture second.

## 5) Solution Narrative (3-Step Journey)

### Step 1: Data Assessment
Goal:
- Make current state visible and quantify opportunity.

Capabilities:
- System and data flow mapping.
- Dependency and bottleneck analysis.
- Data ownership and metric definition review.
- Prioritized roadmap and ROI framing.

Promise:
- Clear roadmap in 2-4 weeks.

### Step 2: Data Foundation
Goal:
- Build durable data infrastructure for reliable operations and analytics.

Capabilities:
- Modern platform architecture (cloud or hybrid).
- Data lake/warehouse setup.
- Pipeline orchestration and reliability controls.
- Governance framework and quality controls.

Promise:
- Trusted, reusable data layer across teams and systems.

### Step 3: Data Products
Goal:
- Convert data foundation into measurable business value.

Capabilities:
- Internal and external data APIs.
- Data-as-a-Service offerings.
- Decision-support and analytics products.
- Revenue and efficiency use cases.

Promise:
- Data that contributes to growth, not just reporting.

## 6) Value Propositions

For executives:
- Faster strategic decisions with trusted numbers.

For operations:
- Less manual work and fewer reporting delays.

For data teams:
- Stable architecture, reusable pipelines, clearer ownership.

For the business:
- Better performance and new revenue pathways from data.

## 7) Differentiators

Pollen Data is different because:
- We build business capabilities, not dashboard-only outputs.
- We continue through execution and productization, not just assessment.
- We prioritize ROI and decision impact, not pipeline volume.
- We keep architecture pragmatic and vendor-neutral.

## 9) Priority Use Cases

Use case 1: Mobility/transport optimization
- Reduce delays, improve planning, and optimize utilization.

Use case 2: Operations efficiency
- Identify bottlenecks, automate handoffs, and cut manual reconciliation.

Use case 3: Revenue generation from data
- Package data capabilities into internal or external value products.

## 11) Messaging Rules (Do / Avoid)

Do:
- Lead with business outcomes.
- Use concrete language and short sections.
- Show progression and momentum (assessment to platform to products).
- Tie every claim to a business effect.

Avoid:
- Generic "we provide solutions" statements.
- Long technical explanations on first read.
- Service-heavy language that hides outcomes.
- Vague transformation claims without mechanism.

## 12) Voice and Tone

Voice attributes:
- Clear
- Credible
- Practical
- Outcome-focused

Writing style:
- Short paragraphs.
- Active voice.
- Specific nouns and verbs.
- Minimal jargon unless immediately clarified.

## 13) CTA Strategy

Primary CTA (site-wide):
- Start Your Data Assessment

Primary CTA promise:
- Get a clear roadmap in 2-4 weeks.

Secondary CTAs:
- See the 3-step approach.
- Explore use cases.
- Talk to a data strategist.

Conversion intent:
- Move visitors from awareness to qualified conversation with explicit business context.

## 14) EN/HE Market Alignment Rules

Keep consistent across languages:
- Core positioning.
- 3-step journey.
- Outcome-first narrative.
- Primary CTA intent.

Allow localization differences:
- Industry examples.
- Pricing sensitivity cues.
- Proof points and case framing.
- Cultural tone and buying journey friction points.

## 15) Content QA Checklist

Every new section/page must pass all checks:
- States a business problem clearly.
- Presents a concrete capability or step.
- Connects capability to measurable value.
- Uses approved positioning language.
- Includes a relevant CTA.
- Avoids banned generic copy patterns.

## 16) Governance

Owner:
- Product marketing and website owner.

Update cadence:
- Monthly review or when strategy changes.

Mandatory update triggers:
- New product capability release.
- Messaging or ICP change.
- New market/language rollout.
- Major conversion or pipeline performance shift.
